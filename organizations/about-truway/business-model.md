# Business model

## Open core

| Layer | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Proof of Spend**, the standard | Open (Apache-2.0) | Governments and funders integrate with a public format, not with a vendor |
| **`budget-treasury`**, the reference contract | Open (Apache-2.0) | Anyone can audit the rules that protect the money |
| **The Truway app**: capture, approvals, reports, the public explorer | Closed source | This is where the product work lives |
| **These docs** | Open (CC BY 4.0) | Reuse them, cite them, improve them |

Being open does not lock anyone in: a public record that follows the standard can be read without
Truway, and data can be exported at any time.

## Who the customer is

| Role | What they get | Their part |
|---|---|---|
| **Executing organisation** | Accounts for a contract without retyping invoices; one report for every funder | Daily user |
| **Government agency** | A supervisor who approves with evidence; a public file for oversight | Champion and approver |
| **Funders and international cooperation** | Execution per funding source, as it happens | Finance programmes that require traceability |

## How we start

A **90-day pilot at no cost to the agency**: one contract, its supervisor and its executing
organisation. See [The 90-day pilot](../public-sector/pilot.md).

## How it grows

- **More contracts per agency**, then more agencies.
- **Country packs**: chart of accounts, electronic invoice format, exchange rate and local
  payment rails. Colombia first, with a generic IFRS pack; El Salvador and Peru are candidates.
- **North star metric:** pesos traced end to end.

Prices are not published yet. They will be, once they are final.
