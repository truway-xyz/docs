# The problem

Public money is often executed by third parties: non-profits, federations, universities and
operators that run programmes on behalf of a government agency. Accounting for that money is slow,
manual and hard to check.

## What officials told us

In October 2026, public officials at a regional workshop for Colombia's National Development Plan
described the problem in their own words:

- They asked for *"an interoperable system that links the formulation, budget allocation,
  contracting, execution and evaluation"* of projects funded with public resources.
- They described public procurement without traceability and *"without interoperability between
  municipalities"*.
- They asked for *"price control mechanisms that strengthen the proper use of resources"*.
- They pointed out that many municipalities have low capacity and poor connectivity.
- They asked for technology that supports citizen oversight.

## What executing organisations told us

We interviewed more than 11 organisations in more than 5 countries. The same pains came back:

- Typing invoices and payment data into spreadsheets, again, for every report.
- Reporting financial and technical execution separately.
- Funders who want to see execution as it happens, not months later.
- A different report for every funder.

## Why existing tools fall short

| Tool | What it does well | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Public procurement portals | Publish the contract | How the money was spent after signing |
| The agency's budget system | Records what the agency paid | What the executing organisation bought with it |
| The organisation's accounting software | Books its expenses | Nobody outside can check it without asking |
| Spreadsheets and email | Flexible | No control, no history, easy to change |

No single system can tell whether **the same invoice was reported twice**, in two contracts or two
municipalities. A shared public record can.
